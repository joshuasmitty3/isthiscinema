title: nolans achilles heel
date: 2026-09-07
---

Every shot in this movie looks like a still from an epic film. Compare that to Fincher or Denis Villeneuve, whose shots often look more like photographs.

The things Nolan has gotten really good at: thoughtful use of CGI, the slow buildup, the "meanwhile back at the ranch" cutting, the reveal of a plan or secret, and especially the third-act buildup. He's also just incredibly good at endings. He never lets a movie fade out. His endings land right at the exact right moment, every time.

All of that shines in this movie. There's also his usual style of dialogue: epic, almost philosophical, and it nearly breaks the fourth wall with how general it gets. It rarely sounds like one character actually talking to another. It sounds more like a monologue aimed at the audience. That's part of what makes his dialogue and music cues hit so hard: he writes lines that feel intense and singular, like they only have one possible meaning.

But that same strength is also my biggest issue with his movies. A lot of his dialogue doesn't feel organic to the character. Even when a line is technically something that character would say, the way he cuts scenes doesn't let it breathe. He's been doing this since at least The Dark Knight. There's a choppiness to it, like he's so eager to get to the point of the scene, to drop the next clue or knock down the next domino, that he never lets characters just talk. No frivolous, meaningful back and forth. No scenes that exist just to let two people be in a room together.

This is where I land on the Michael Bay comparison. Bay and Nolan are both incredible at big, high-stakes action filmmaking, but neither is that good at simple storytelling, at just two characters talking in a quiet, thoughtful way.

Nolan has this habit, what the kids would call "intensity maxing," where everything is dialed to the highest possible stakes at all times. It reminds me of Kendrick's music: no casual buildup, no small reveals, no room to just sit in a moment. Everything is climbing toward the most intense point possible. Take the scene in Interstellar where Cooper watches years of video messages from his kids after being gone seven years and breaks down crying. Nobody would say Nolan can't direct emotion after watching that scene. He's not just an action guy like Bay. But the emotional dial is always turned to ten, and this movie has that same issue. The best acting moments are always the most intense ones, which means the smaller scenes, the exposition, the connective tissue, end up feeling rushed and clunky.

He's also had this problem for years: muffled dialogue. People brought this up a lot with Tenet, maybe the worst offender. I don't know if it's the IMAX cameras or just a mixing issue, but there's a general feeling that the dialogue gets rushed and undercooked. That's what I mean by the building blocks. Nolan struggles to build a simple scene where two characters just talk and it feels natural. Somehow, for as great a filmmaker as he is, a quiet scene with good staging, lighting, and camera placement is one of the hardest things for him to pull off.
