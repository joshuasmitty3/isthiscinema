---
title: nolans achilles heel
date: 2026-09-14
---

when you watch a nolan movie, you already know what you're in for. you gotta watch it in the theaters, in its proper state, and the closer it is to opening night, the further from spoilers. did he use cgi or shoot everything practical? was a new lighting rig or camera lens invented? who was allowed to read the script before signing on? is the movie's runtime an easter egg? but the core nolan-ism is taken from the book of m. night shyamalan: every movie must have a reveal. the point of the non-sequential plot, the perpetual meanwhile-back-at-the-ranch cuts, the thematic exposition, is all in service of a cinematic ta-da. he embeds the theme throughout the movie, and like breadcrumbs, each scene carries the audience to the next clue until we reach the third act, and then, typically moments before credits roll, the reveal snaps into focus. when well-executed, when he sticks the landing, and he doesn't always, but when he does, it really works. his endings have been perfect. no fat. boom, then credits.

however, it's his dedication to that catharsis that draws nolan's auteur closer to michael bay or zack snyder (away from spielberg or fincher). nolan outclasses them in every category (maybe not in fun-ness), and yet the venn diagram closes around a single shared vice, an addiction to the dopamine hit. bay and snyder chase it through cgi explosions and slow-motion hero shots, empty calories dressed as awe, while the english filmmaker reaches for something higher. his films have a point.

while promoting the odyssey, nolan addressed some of his critics by saying, "because to be able to identify the mechanism does not invalidate the mechanism... that's not how storytelling works". this is true. the reveal, even when you know it is coming...in fact, **because** you know it is coming, generates goosebumps. the issue is not in execution, it's the pacing.

because the odyssey, like other nolan films, slowly builds to its climax, it feels patient, even thoughtful at times, but the gravitational pull of his impending reveal is so strong, so telegraphed, that despite not knowing how the dominoes will fall, we all know it's coming and early scenes especially are only mechanisms to get us there. there's nothing frivolous, no character quirks, no jokes, no breathing room between scenes or even within moments. there is only the plot. nolan's all-seeing, all-knowing plot. it marches forward, perfectly timed and relentless. their pace is *intensitymaxxing*. no space to breathe. bay's pacing prefers action sequence, then comedy, and sometimes sex. snyder prefers action then sex and sometimes comedy, so a quiet scene where two characters talk or there's a subtle tension or a nuanced emotion is a struggle. it must be loud. nolan's movies are filled with emotion, but always turned to a 10. a father on a spaceship light years away, weeping as he watches tapes of his "now" grown kids. goosebumps. even scenes that aren't big with emotions or revealing need to be amplified. the mounting drums and horns of zimmer or ludwig against the whispers of expositional dialogue. and all without pause because there is no room for interpretation. there is only the set-up and the punchline. chris rock once said about bad jokes, "it's not working because the audience does not understand the premise…if i set this premise up right, this joke will always work".

the other side-effect of his plot-heaviness is the lack of strong visual language. he promotes celluloid-packed film stocks and theatrical experiences, but within the massive images and spectacular clarity, there isn't much beauty in the odyssey. there's epicness, there's spectacle, there's a behind-the-scenes awareness of nolan's practicals-over-vfx approach, but if you freeze any frame and look at the image. let it stand alone for a moment and breathe. that 1/24th of a second is nothing noteworthy. there are many auteurs who shoot with a sense of photography and there is not a right way to make movies, but for a filmmaker who pushes the transcendent experience of sitting in a dark space waiting to dream, it is odd that he is so disinterested in photographic imagery. you don't even need to watch the movie to see what i mean. watch [this trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mzw2ttJD2qQ), then watch [this one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NdvqHc56lE0). colors, composition, lighting, framing, all take a backseat in nolan's universe.

fincher once said he likes to move the camera as though it has some omniscient awareness of what's going to happen. removed from the characters' motivations, their struggles and desires, it pans and zooms and dollies and cuts with detachment. it already knows what will happen and knows there's no way for the characters to realize it. they're in a greek tragedy, and only they don't know it.

having said all that, the odyssey is still one of the greatest stories ever told and it's probably nolan's best since interstellar. **4/5**
